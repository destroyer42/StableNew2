### UX-DEC-071 — Hybrid Context-Aware Evidence Reconciliation

**OWNER CONFIRMED**

Analyze methodological differences and source quality before combining evidence. Preserve contradictory findings, weight genuinely comparable studies, explain uncertainty and recommend targeted validation when disagreement could affect a decision. Operators approve consequential recommendation or profile changes.

#### Confirmed requirements

1. Assess methodological comparability before aggregating findings.
2. Evaluate source quality, provenance and relevant qualifications.
3. Preserve contradictory results and original research conditions.
4. Weight genuinely comparable studies using defensible methods.
5. Explain material uncertainty and disagreement.
6. Distinguish apparent conflicts caused by different experimental conditions from genuine conflicting findings.
7. Propose targeted validation when disagreement could materially affect decisions.
8. Require operator approval for consequential recommendation changes.
9. Preserve separate learned-profile promotion approval.
10. Avoid treating imported observations or duplicate samples as independently qualified local evidence.
11. Preserve canonical experimental History and original measurements.
12. Respect existing research, authorization and physical-execution boundaries.

#### Proposed detailed design — pending repository validation

**Evidence comparison**

Identify the specific question each study attempted to answer.

Compare checkpoint identity, backend version, effective settings, prompt adaptations, content categories, resolutions, trial counts, evaluator versions, hardware conditions and outcome definitions.

Studies addressing different questions should not be forcibly merged.

**Source qualification**

Assess the provenance and reliability of each evidence source.

Distinguish verified local observations, independently qualified imported observations, published findings and insufficiently documented research.

Source origin alone should not determine scientific validity.

**Comparability assessment**

Classify findings as directly comparable, comparable with documented adjustments, conditionally relevant or insufficiently comparable.

Explain which dimensions prevent aggregation.

**Duplicate and dependent evidence**

Recognize repeated imports, shared source datasets, duplicate experiment records and nonindependent observations.

Do not count copied results as additional independent confirmation.

**Evidence weighting**

Use statistically and methodologically defensible weighting when sufficient comparable evidence exists.

Consider study quality, precision, measurement reliability, sample coverage and independence.

Do not assume that a large but poorly controlled study necessarily outweighs a smaller, well-controlled one.

**Conditional conclusions**

When differing conditions explain apparent conflicts, preserve findings tied to those conditions.

For example, recommendations may legitimately differ between portrait and landscape tasks, checkpoints, hardware environments or optimization objectives.

**Genuine disagreements**

When comparable studies produce materially conflicting results, preserve their individual findings and indicate the unresolved disagreement.

Do not automatically select the newest, most favorable or most numerous result.

**Uncertainty**

Represent uncertainty using methods appropriate to the evidence.

Quantitative intervals or probabilities require valid statistical assumptions and sufficient data.

Otherwise use clear qualitative limitations rather than invented numerical confidence.

**Technical quality and human preference**

Keep objective or qualified technical measures separate from personalized preference judgments.

Different conclusions across these dimensions may express tradeoffs rather than contradictory research.

**Targeted validation**

Identify which uncertainty materially affects a pending recommendation, qualification or profile decision.

Propose focused experiments or reevaluation of existing artifacts sufficient to distinguish plausible explanations.

Avoid unnecessarily repeating entire characterization campaigns.

**Operator involvement**

Present the conflicting findings, supporting evidence, applicable conditions, proposed interpretation and available decisions.

Require explicit operator approval for consequential recommendation or learned-profile changes where the established policy requires it.

Operator preference may govern product choices but must not be represented as proof that one scientific finding is objectively correct.

**Research conclusions**

Preserve qualified, conditional and unresolved conclusions in the research workspace established by UX-DEC-054.

Maintain references to original evidence and avoid rewriting historical observations.

**Ranking stability**

When contradictory evidence weakens a model recommendation, disclose the reason and retain historical provenance.

Do not silently change permanent profiles or manually selected checkpoints.

**Architecture and safety**

Reconciliation is an analytical operation over existing research evidence.

Any additional generation must follow the canonical NJR, JobService, SQLite queue and PipelineRunner path.

The October 12, 2026 physical GPU/Forge/SVD hold remains in effect. Research reconciliation does not authorize physical generation, qualification or backend restarts.

#### Proposed acceptance scenarios

A. Identify two apparently contradictory studies using different checkpoint revisions and preserve their separate conclusions.

B. Recognize that portrait and landscape research results may support different settings.

C. Combine genuinely comparable independent studies using documented evidence-weighting rules.

D. Avoid double-counting repeated observations imported through multiple research packages.

E. Retain contradictory evidence when current research cannot resolve the disagreement.

F. Identify when hardware differences prevent direct performance comparison.

G. Distinguish an AI technical-quality assessment from a human preference judgment.

H. Describe uncertainty accurately without fabricating confidence percentages.

I. Recommend targeted experiments when disagreement would change the preferred sampler or checkpoint.

J. Prefer reassessment of existing artifacts when additional generation is unnecessary.

K. Explain a proposed recommendation change using the relevant new and historical evidence.

L. Require appropriate approval before consequential recommendation changes or learned-profile promotion.

M. Preserve original NJRs, artifact lineage and research records.

N. Prevent proposed validation from bypassing the physical-execution hold.
