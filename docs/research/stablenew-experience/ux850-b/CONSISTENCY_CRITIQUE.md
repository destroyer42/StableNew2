## Method 1 — alternative-first counterproposal

Assume N2's journey guide is preferable: the application already has task groups, yet five journeys cross them. A visible next-step guide could prevent operators losing the path between Prompt and Pipeline, clip and assembly, or findings and Create. N1's contextual labels may be too weak; its source-specific Library remains fragmented.

Countercheck N2 against the exact current owners: a linear step cannot truthfully represent a Storyboard readiness summary as executable approval, nor publication as Create application. To stay correct the guide needs branching, freshness, destination/lifetime checks and derived progress without independent state. It also consumes space in the very short-window cases not yet measured. N1 reduces that coordination while preserving all advanced routes. Therefore retain N1 as the **research recommendation**, with N2 a viable contingent alternative, and expose the unresolved discovery limitation instead of claiming a unified catalog.

**Falsification:** in a separately authorized operator study, compare task completion, error/recovery and advanced-control discovery across N1/N2 at supported viewports, with unchanged tasks/data. No assumed timing target or sample success denominator exists here. If users still cannot find existing assets with N1, approve a bounded common-browser contract or reconsider N2; do not conclude that new persistence is automatically necessary.

## Residual limits and framework improvement

No operator tasks, installed-resource checks, GUI execution, artifact-quality results, full store adversarial audit or independent critique were performed. General handoff cancellation is a target behavior, not established parity. N1 can improve coherence while leaving unified discovery incomplete. Evidence does not show it will reduce time or errors.

For the next design/implementation contract, carry one identity/freshness table alongside each screen route and name what can actually be persisted. This catches the most consequential paper mistakes here: a summary mistaken for frozen approval, a source helper mistaken for transactional placement, and publication mistaken for application. Keep implementation readiness, demonstration, qualification and usability benefit separate; A's all-row evidence remains the baseline rather than being upgraded by these diagrams.
